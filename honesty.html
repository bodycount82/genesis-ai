These are not disclaimers to wave past. They are precise statements of what the mechanism is not.

Analogy ≠ evidence

Not “closest to biological memory”

Genesis shares useful design ideas with biological memory—salience gating, recall-strengthening, consolidation, and forgetting. Proximity needs evidence it does not yet have.

Comparison ≠ benchmark

Not “categorically better”

Until shared tests exist for Mem0, Zep, Letta, and Genesis, the honest statement is: a different architecture with different trade-offs.

Thresholded

Contradictions are similarity-based

The newer fact supersedes by text similarity—not by a temporal truth engine. Contradictions can survive until they are near enough to collide.

Degraded mode

Hashing is not semantic memory

Without the embedding model, retrieval becomes stable and keyword-like, but not meaning-aware. It is a fallback and is labeled as one.

Order-sensitive

Clustering is greedy

The order experiences arrive can change which rules are distilled. The pass is effective; it is not optimal.

Known bias

Lexical logic is English-centric

Keyword overlap and much of the salience language assume English structure. Other languages are scored less faithfully.

Graded protection

Flashbulbs are not immortal

A flashbulb fades more slowly. It does not live forever. Everything in this system is mortal by design.

Scope boundary

Deleting is not erasing

Removing a memory record does not scrub its chat transcript or rotating backups. Deletion is not a data-forensics guarantee.

A common misunderstanding

Sleep does not lift
everything up.

Consolidation is selective, not wholesale. A single experience never becomes a rule.

Consolidation, accuratelyselective

Recent episodes are replayed and ranked. Only genuinely similar experiences form a cluster. From a multi-episode cluster, one rule or concept may be distilled. Survivors reinforce; the rest are folded or left to fade.
